## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 16. Today your mission is to choose tax software. Choose the operating system behind every return. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Compare tax software against workflow, security, support, integration, pricing, and growth requirements before signing.

Tax software affects preparation speed, diagnostics, e-filing, document handling, review, reporting, and the client experience.

Evaluate total cost, supported return types, multi-user controls, training, security, bank-product integration, conversion support, uptime, and service response. A familiar demo is not a substitute for a documented comparison.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on workflow. Preparation, diagnostics, review, e-file, and document flow. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on security. Access control, encryption, backups, and audit history. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on support. Training, conversion, availability, and escalation. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on scale. Users, locations, integrations, pricing, and future capacity. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: Do not choose on price alone. A weak workflow, poor support, or missing security controls can cost more than the license. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Software Scorecard. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Name the software choice you are evaluating and the three questions the vendor must answer before purchase. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Select a Bank Product Partner. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
