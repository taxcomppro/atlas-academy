## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 23. Today your mission is to install review and fraud safeguards. Detect inconsistencies before they become filed returns. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Add identity verification, consistency checks, reviewer checkpoints, red-flag escalation, and documented resolution to the workflow.

Fraud prevention is most effective when controls operate before submission: identity checks, source reconciliation, cross-year comparisons, duplicate detection, bank-information verification, and review of unusual results.

Define red flags, who investigates them, what evidence clears them, and when the office must pause or decline work. Volume or deadlines must never override an unresolved risk.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on prevent. Identity, access, source, and payment controls. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on detect. Diagnostics, comparisons, duplicates, and unusual-result checks. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on escalate. Pause, reviewer assignment, investigation, and decline rules. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on evidence. Resolution notes, approvals, corrections, and retained support. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: A safeguard that can be skipped without review is only a suggestion. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Fraud Safeguard Map. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Name the red flag that will automatically stop a return in your office and identify who can release it. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Design Client Intake and Workflow. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
