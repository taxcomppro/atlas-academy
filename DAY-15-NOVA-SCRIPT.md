# Day 15 - Nova Production Script

**Lesson:** Brand Your Tax Office
**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, professional and conversational
**Target length:** 3-5 minutes
**Narration:** 431 words; approximately 3.2 minutes at 135 words per minute
**Toolkit source:** pages 50-52

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Branding is the consistent promise clients experience at every point of contact.

### Scene 2 - What to do

Start with the audience you serve and the standard you intend to deliver. Define who the office is for, what problems it helps solve, and why clients should trust the process. Translate that promise into a recognizable identity: the approved business name, logo, colors, typography, imagery, and tone. Apply the same system to the website, storefront, social profiles, forms, email, office environment, appointment process, and follow-up.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Define the promise. Position the office around a clear audience, service standard, and reason clients should trust the experience. Build a consistent identity. Use an approved name, logo, color palette, typography, tone, and imagery consistently across physical and digital touchpoints. Document the message. Create concise descriptions of the office, services, process, credentials, security practices, and appointment method. Avoid risky claims. Do not promise outcomes, exaggerate credentials, misuse official marks, or use refund language that could mislead clients.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Write clear descriptions of your services, process, credentials, security practices, and appointment method. Avoid guarantees, exaggerated credentials, misuse of official marks, and refund or outcome language that could mislead clients. Your mission is to complete the brand foundation and document the rules that keep it consistent. A trustworthy brand is not decoration; it is a clear expectation that the operation must deliver. In your Day 15 action plan, document the following. Target audience: Primary client group and needs. Brand promise: The dependable experience clients should expect. Positioning statement: Who you serve, what you provide, and why it matters. Voice and personality: Three to five characteristics. Visual standards: Logo, colors, typography, imagery, and usage rules. Core touchpoints: Website, signs, social profiles, forms, email, office, and follow-up.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.

## HeyGen script framing

This script is a concept and theme to convey - not a verbatim transcript. You have full creative freedom to expand, elaborate, add examples, and fill the duration naturally. Do not pad with silence or pauses.

## Production direction

Use the selected Nova presenter with a confident, practical delivery. Keep the Atlas Academy navy, blue, white, and gold palette. Use clean motion graphics for checklists and process steps, plus restrained stock office footage only when it improves understanding.
