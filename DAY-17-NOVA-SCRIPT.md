## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 17. Today your mission is to select a bank product partner. Protect clients while evaluating funding products. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Compare fees, disclosures, reliability, compliance controls, funding timelines, and software integration before choosing a partner.

Bank products may provide refund-transfer or advance options, but they introduce fees, disclosures, eligibility rules, and operational responsibilities.

Evaluate transparent pricing, client consent, funding reliability, issue resolution, fraud controls, training, compliance monitoring, and direct integration with your selected tax software.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on products. Available products, eligibility, timing, and limits. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on fees. Client and preparer costs with clear disclosures. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on safeguards. Consent, fraud prevention, privacy, and compliance controls. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on operations. Software integration, funding support, and issue escalation. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: Convenience does not remove disclosure duties. Clients must understand costs, alternatives, and product terms. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Partner Fit Review. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Record the partner under review and the disclosure standard your office will use with every client. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Complete Vendor Due Diligence. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
