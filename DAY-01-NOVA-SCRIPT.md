### Scene 1 - Your legal foundation

Welcome to Day 1. Today, you are choosing the legal foundation for your tax office.

Your business structure affects how the office is recognized, how income is reported, how ownership decisions are made, and how well your personal assets may be separated from business obligations. It can also affect banking credibility, your ability to add partners or investors, and the systems you will need as the office grows.

This is not a paperwork shortcut. It is a protection and operating decision.

### Scene 2 - The five questions

Before comparing entity types, answer five questions.

Will you own the business alone or with someone else? How much personal liability are you willing to accept? Do you expect to hire, add locations, or bring in investors? How much administrative structure can you maintain consistently? And what tax treatment makes sense for your projected income and compensation plan?

You do not need final answers yet. Your mission is to organize the facts a qualified attorney, accountant, or tax professional will need.

### Scene 3 - Sole proprietorship

A sole proprietorship is generally the simplest way to begin operating. It has minimal formation requirements, and the owner keeps full control. Business income is generally reported on the owner's individual return.

The tradeoff is important: there is no separate legal entity between the owner and the business. That can leave the owner personally responsible for business debts, lawsuits, or penalties. Simplicity does not equal protection.

### Scene 4 - Limited liability company

An LLC creates a legal entity separate from its owner or owners when it is properly formed and maintained. It is widely used by small service businesses because it can balance liability protection, operational flexibility, and manageable formalities.

An LLC also offers flexibility in tax classification. A single-member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income-tax purposes unless it elects otherwise, while a multi-member LLC is generally treated as a partnership unless another election is made. An LLC may later elect corporate tax treatment when appropriate.

The important words are properly formed and maintained. Separate banking, accurate records, and disciplined operations matter.

### Scene 5 - Corporation

A corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders. It can offer strong liability protection, structured ownership, and a clearer path for adding investors or raising capital.

That structure comes with more administration. Corporations generally require stronger governance, recordkeeping, and ongoing formalities. Tax treatment also differs between C corporations and S corporations, so the election should match the owner's income, compensation, and growth strategy.

### Scene 6 - Protection has limits

No entity eliminates professional responsibility. A tax preparer can still be personally responsible for their own negligence, fraud, or failure to follow due-diligence rules.

Legal structure must work alongside professional liability insurance, sound compliance systems, separate business finances, accurate records, and ethical conduct. Protection is a system, not a filing receipt.

### Scene 7 - Your Day 1 decision

Complete the Business Structure Decision Worksheet. Compare the three common structures, record your ownership and growth plans, identify your liability concerns, and list the questions you need a professional to answer.

Then choose a working direction: sole proprietor, LLC, corporation, or professional review needed. A working direction is not a final filing decision. Do not form or elect an entity until you have checked your state requirements and obtained advice appropriate to your situation.

When your worksheet and checkpoint are complete, Day 2 will unlock. Tomorrow, we will compare the entity types in more detail and test your working direction against your long-term plans.
