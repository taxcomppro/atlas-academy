**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 30. Today your mission is to open your tax office. Launch with discipline, visibility, and a steady operating rhythm. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Open with a documented announcement, clear client standard, first-week controls, daily review rhythm, and a plan for continuous improvement.

A professional launch combines clear hours and contact information, a consistent client welcome, visible privacy practices, organized workspaces, tested technology, reliable payments, and trained staff.

Use opening and closing checklists, daily status huddles, workload monitoring, issue escalation, quality checks, client follow-up, and a first-week debrief. Protect the standards built during the prior twenty-nine missions.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on welcome. Hours, arrival, privacy, service standard, and communication. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on operate. Daily schedule, workload, status, handoffs, and payments. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on protect. Security, review, escalation, backups, and closing controls. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on improve. Feedback, defect review, coaching, priorities, and next-week plan. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: Do not let opening-week urgency erase the compliance, security, and review gates that made the office ready. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Opening Week Command Plan. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Write your opening announcement, client-service standard, and the three numbers you will review every day during launch week. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

Carry these documented standards into opening week and continuous improvement. Protect the controls you built, review the first week honestly, and correct issues before they become habits. Your office is not finished growing; it is ready to operate with intention.

## HeyGen script framing

This script is a concept and theme to convey - not a verbatim transcript. You have full creative freedom to expand, elaborate, add examples, and fill the duration naturally. Do not pad with silence or pauses.
