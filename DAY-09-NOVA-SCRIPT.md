# Day 9 - Nova Production Script

**Lesson:** Begin EFIN and IRS Registration
**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, professional and conversational
**Target length:** 3-5 minutes
**Narration:** 426 words; approximately 3.2 minutes at 135 words per minute
**Toolkit source:** pages 33-34

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Your PTIN authorizes you to act as a paid preparer when required. The EFIN supports the office's ability to participate in electronic filing.

### Scene 2 - What to do

These identifiers are not interchangeable. The EFIN application is tied to the e-file provider, the legal business information, the location, and the people responsible for the operation. Begin by confirming the entity name, EIN, physical location, principals, and responsible officials. Establish secure IRS e-services access and review the current e-file provider application instructions. Depending on the role and credentials involved, suitability checks or fingerprinting may apply.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Understand the distinction. A PTIN identifies an individual paid preparer. An EFIN identifies an authorized electronic return originator or provider location. Set up e-services. Create and secure the appropriate IRS online accounts before starting the e-file provider application. Identify responsible officials. List principals and responsible officials accurately and prepare for identity, suitability, and fingerprinting steps when applicable. Plan for lead time. The application involves IRS review. Begin early, monitor correspondence, and keep the application details current.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Treat the application as a controlled project. Assign an owner, preserve every submission and notice, monitor the account, and respond promptly to IRS requests. Changes in ownership, responsible officials, or location may also require updates. Your mission is to build the readiness packet before the official application begins. Accurate roles and matching records reduce avoidable delays. In your Day 9 action plan, document the following. Legal entity and EIN: Confirm exact matching information. Business location: Physical address and intended e-file location. Principals: Owners or controlling persons. Responsible officials: Individuals responsible for e-file compliance. Suitability and fingerprinting: Requirements to confirm for each person. Application monitoring: Owner, account access, and correspondence plan.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.
