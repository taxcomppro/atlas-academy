# Day 24 - Nova Production Script

**Lesson:** Design Client Intake and Workflow
**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, professional and conversational
**Target length:** 3-5 minutes

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 24. Today your mission is to design client intake and workflow. Give every client and every file a clear path. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Map the client journey from first contact through intake, preparation, review, signature, filing, payment, delivery, and retention.

A defined workflow reduces lost documents, unclear ownership, duplicated effort, missed follow-up, and inconsistent client communication.

Use a central status system, assigned handoffs, standardized messages, scheduling rules, document checkpoints, review gates, and a final closeout process. Every file should always have an owner, status, next action, and due date.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on enter. Inquiry, scheduling, engagement, identity, and intake. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on prepare. Document collection, questions, preparation, and status. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on review. Quality control, corrections, approval, and signature. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on close. E-file, payment, delivery, retention, and follow-up. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: If the next action depends on someone remembering a conversation, the workflow needs a recorded handoff. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Client Journey Map. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Write the status labels your team will use and define what must be true before a file moves forward. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Standardize Documentation and Quality Review. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
