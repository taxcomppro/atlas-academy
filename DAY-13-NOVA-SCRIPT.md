# Day 13 - Nova Production Script

**Lesson:** Plan Signage and Window Displays
**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, professional and conversational
**Target length:** 3-5 minutes
**Narration:** 423 words; approximately 3.1 minutes at 135 words per minute
**Toolkit source:** pages 46

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Your storefront should answer three questions quickly: who you are, what you do, and how a client can reach you.

### Scene 2 - What to do

Before ordering anything, confirm landlord rules, center standards, local sign codes, permits, lighting restrictions, size limits, placement, and installation requirements. Lead with a readable business name and consistent brand. Select the few services that matter most to your target clients. A window crowded with every offering, promotion, and promise becomes harder to understand and easier to distrust.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Confirm permissions. Review landlord, center, municipal, sign-code, permit, lighting, size, placement, and installation requirements before ordering. Lead with the brand. Make the legal or approved public business name readable and consistent with the office's identity. Prioritize services. Communicate only the most important offerings. Too many messages make the window harder to understand. Protect visibility and trust. Avoid blocking sightlines or covering the entire storefront. Use claims, pricing, and refund-related language carefully and accurately.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Preserve sightlines and professional appearance. Test visibility during the day and at night, from the directions clients actually approach. Include useful information such as hours, phone, website, or appointment instructions without displaying confidential information. Your mission is to prepare a signage brief that a vendor can price and a landlord or local authority can review. Clear and accurate beats loud and cluttered. In your Day 13 action plan, document the following. Primary exterior identifier: Business name and logo placement. Priority services: Three to five messages at most. Window information: Hours, phone, website, appointment method. Approvals and permits: Landlord, property, municipal, and installation. Visibility plan: Day, night, distance, traffic direction, and accessibility. Accuracy and compliance review: Claims, prices, refund language, and required disclosures.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.

## HeyGen script framing

This script is a concept and theme to convey - not a verbatim transcript. You have full creative freedom to expand, elaborate, add examples, and fill the duration naturally. Do not pad with silence or pauses.

## Production direction

Use the selected Nova presenter with a confident, practical delivery. Keep the Atlas Academy navy, blue, white, and gold palette. Use clean motion graphics for checklists and process steps, plus restrained stock office footage only when it improves understanding. Do not display sensitive identification numbers, tax return data, account credentials, or invented government forms.
