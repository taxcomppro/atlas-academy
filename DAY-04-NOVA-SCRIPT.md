## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Today we are securing the federal business identity for your tax office: the Employer Identification Number, or EIN.

### Scene 2 - What to do

Before you open the application, assemble the legal business name, any trade name, your entity type, physical and mailing addresses, ownership details, the responsible party, and the reason you are applying. These answers should be consistent with your formation records and the way the business will actually operate. Use the official IRS channel. The IRS does not charge a fee to issue an EIN, so be cautious with third-party sites that look official and charge for the application. When eligible, the online application is generally the fastest route, but the IRS also provides alternative methods when the online process does not fit.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Prepare first. Confirm the legal name, trade name, entity type, responsible party, addresses, ownership details, and reason for applying before beginning. Use the official IRS channel. The IRS online EIN application is generally the fastest option when eligibility requirements are met. The IRS does not charge an EIN application fee. Answer consistently. The entity choice and ownership information should match your formation documents and operating reality. Save the notice. Preserve the EIN confirmation notice digitally and in the startup record file. Banks, payroll providers, tax agencies, and vendors may request it.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

After the EIN is issued, save the confirmation immediately. Keep a secure digital copy and a printed copy in your startup record file. You may need that notice when opening a business bank account, setting up payroll, registering with tax agencies, or working with vendors. Your mission is to complete the EIN application packet in this lesson, verify every answer, and document any question that requires professional guidance. Accuracy now prevents mismatched records later. In your Day 4 action plan, document the following. Legal business name: Exactly as shown on formation records. Trade name or DBA: If applicable. Entity type: Sole proprietor, LLC, corporation, partnership, or other. Responsible party: Name and relationship to the business. Business address: Physical and mailing address. Reason for applying: New business, banking, employees, entity change, or other.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.
