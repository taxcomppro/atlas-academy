TOOLKIT LESSON

Three legal structures and one tax-election profile

The toolkit introduces sole proprietorships, limited liability companies, and corporations as common legal structures. This lesson also brings the S Corporation into the comparison because it is a common tax profile for eligible small businesses. An S Corporation is generally a federal tax election made by an eligible corporation or other eligible entity - not a fourth state-law entity type.

SOLE PROPRIETORSimpleEasy to begin; highest personal-risk tradeoff.
LLCBalancedProtection and flexibility with manageable formalities.
C CORPORATIONStructuredFormal ownership, entity-level taxation, and scaling potential.
S CORPORATIONTax ElectionPass-through federal taxation for an eligible entity, with payroll and compliance duties.
The most popular choice is not automatically your best choice.

Your facts, state requirements, projected income, ownership plan, and ability to maintain the entity must drive the decision.

S Corporation status does not create liability protection by itself.

The underlying LLC or corporation provides the legal structure. The S election changes eligible federal tax treatment and adds rules, filings, and reasonable-compensation responsibilities.
