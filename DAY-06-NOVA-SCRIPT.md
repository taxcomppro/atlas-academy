# Day 6 - Nova Production Script

**Lesson:** Open Your Business Bank Account
**Presenter:** Nova Grant, Launch Commander
**Format:** 16:9 landscape, professional and conversational
**Target length:** 3-5 minutes
**Narration:** 434 words; approximately 3.2 minutes at 135 words per minute
**Toolkit source:** pages 31-32

## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

A business bank account is one of the clearest operational boundaries you can create.

### Scene 2 - What to do

Use it to receive business income and pay business expenses. Avoid using it as an extension of your personal account. Consistent separation makes bookkeeping cleaner, supports tax reporting, and reinforces the business's independent operation. Before the appointment, ask the institution for its exact document list. You may need approved formation documents, the EIN confirmation, an operating agreement or bylaws, ownership information, personal identification, and a resolution identifying authorized signers.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Maintain separation. Deposit business income and pay business expenses from the business account rather than mixing personal and business funds. Bring the right records. Banks commonly request formation documents, EIN confirmation, governing documents, ownership information, identification, and an authorizing resolution. Compare account controls. Review fees, transaction limits, cash handling, online access, dual controls, alerts, deposits, and integrations. Document authorized users. Record who may access the account, approve transactions, sign checks, or change banking instructions.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Do not compare accounts on the monthly fee alone. Consider transaction limits, cash deposits, remote check capture, ACH and wire capability, alerts, dual approval, user permissions, customer support, and integration with your accounting system. Your mission is to compare the operational controls, prepare the document packet, and record who can access or approve transactions. Financial separation only works when the daily process supports it. In your Day 6 action plan, document the following. Bank or credit union: Institution under consideration. Required documents: Formation, EIN, governance, identification, resolution. Monthly and transaction fees: Include cash, ACH, wires, and card activity. Security controls: Alerts, dual approval, access roles, positive pay, or other. Deposit and payment needs: Cash, checks, ACH, wires, cards, remote deposit. Authorized users: Role and permitted actions.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.
