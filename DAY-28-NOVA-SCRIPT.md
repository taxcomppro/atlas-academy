## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 28. Today your mission is to run the grand opening checklist. Verify the whole office before announcing it is ready. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Confirm legal, IRS, banking, software, security, staffing, facility, workflow, marketing, insurance, and contingency readiness.

A launch depends on connected systems. Entity records, EIN, banking, PTIN and EFIN status, software, security, insurance, staff, policies, signage, supplies, intake, review, payments, and communications must work together.

Use one master checklist with evidence, accountable owners, deadlines, and a clear rule for unresolved critical items. Marking a box without verifying the supporting evidence creates false confidence.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on authority. Legal, IRS, banking, insurance, contracts, and licenses. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on systems. Software, security, equipment, records, and payments. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on people. Staffing, training, roles, schedules, and escalation. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on market. Brand, signage, communications, hours, and launch plan. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: A critical legal, IRS, security, insurance, or workflow gap should delay the affected service until it is resolved. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Launch Readiness Board. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Name the unresolved item most likely to delay launch and assign its owner and deadline. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Complete a Mock Client Session. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
