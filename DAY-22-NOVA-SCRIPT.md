## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Welcome to Day 22. Today your mission is to build audit-ready client files. Make the file explain what happened and why. This mission turns an important launch decision into a documented operating standard that your office can repeat, review, and improve.

### Scene 2 - Why this mission matters

Organize source documents, questionnaires, notes, calculations, review evidence, signatures, and retention controls into one defensible record.

An audit-ready file should allow an authorized reviewer to identify the client, trace the source documents, understand unusual facts, see the inquiries made, and confirm who prepared and reviewed the work.

Standard folders, naming rules, required-document indexes, contemporaneous notes, version control, retention schedules, and restricted access reduce both compliance risk and operational confusion.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

First, focus on identity. Client authorization, identity, and engagement records. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Next, focus on evidence. Source documents, questionnaires, and calculations. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Then, focus on reasoning. Follow-up inquiries, notes, exceptions, and conclusions. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

Finally, focus on control. Review, signatures, retention, access, and final archive. Do not mark this area ready based on intention alone. Record the current status, the accountable owner, the evidence that proves completion, and any dependency that could keep the office from moving forward.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Keep this operating rule in view: If the reasoning only exists in the preparer's memory, the file is incomplete. When something is uncertain, pause the affected decision, identify the official source or qualified professional who can resolve it, and preserve the answer with the mission records. A deadline, sales claim, or busy-season shortcut is not evidence.

Now complete the Client File Architecture. Work through all four mission areas, name the accountable owner, and identify the document, test, approval, or other evidence that will prove each item is ready. Use the action plan to expose gaps early, while they can still be corrected without disrupting a client or filing deadline.

Your professional-review prompt is this: Describe the naming and folder rule that will let another authorized reviewer understand a file without asking the preparer. Write the answer in plain language. List the facts that support it, the questions that remain open, and the date or event that will trigger another review. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy worksheet.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

Before you complete the checkpoint, review the mission from the client's point of view and the office owner's point of view. Ask whether the process is clear, secure, compliant, financially sustainable, and understandable to another trained team member. If the process depends on one person's memory, convert it into a written step, assigned control, or retained record.

When the action plan and checkpoint are complete, continue to Install Review and Fraud Safeguards. Bring the records from this mission forward so the next decision builds on verified work instead of starting over. I will meet you at the next mission.
