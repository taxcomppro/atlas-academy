## Narration script

### Scene 1 - Mission hook

Your structure decision becomes real when the legal entity is properly formed and documented.

### Scene 2 - What to do

Start with the appropriate state authority. Confirm name availability, required designators, filing forms, registered-agent rules, fees, and any publication or licensing requirements. Requirements vary, so use official state information and professional guidance when necessary. Formation is more than submitting one form. Your governing documents should explain who owns the company, who can bind it, how decisions are made, how money moves, and what happens when ownership changes. For an LLC, this is often addressed in an operating agreement. Corporations typically rely on bylaws, resolutions, and documented officer or director actions.

### Scene 3 - Four operating controls

Use this four-part checklist while you work. Confirm availability. Verify the proposed business name and naming requirements with the appropriate state authority before filing. Choose authority carefully. Identify the registered agent, organizers, members, managers, officers, or directors required for your chosen structure. Adopt governing documents. An operating agreement, bylaws, resolutions, or similar records clarify ownership, authority, voting, compensation, and decision-making. Preserve proof. Keep approved formation documents, amendments, annual reports, and state correspondence in the permanent record file.

### Scene 4 - Build the action plan

Once the filing is approved, protect the evidence. Save the stamped formation document, acceptance notice, governing documents, amendments, annual reports, and state correspondence together. These records support banking, licensing, vendor onboarding, and the legal separation you expect the entity to provide. Your mission is to build a state-specific formation checklist and identify every item that needs legal or tax review before you submit. In your Day 5 action plan, document the following. State and filing authority: Secretary of State or other agency. Proposed legal name: Include required designator. Registered agent plan: Name, service, and address requirements. Ownership and management: Members, managers, shareholders, officers, or directors. Governing documents: Operating agreement, bylaws, resolutions, or other. Ongoing state obligations: Annual report, franchise tax, license, publication, or other.

### Scene 5 - Control and completion

As you complete this mission, use a simple three-part control: source, evidence, and owner. Verify the requirement with an official source or a qualified professional. Preserve the document, confirmation, or decision that supports your work. Then assign the next action and due date to a specific person. Do not place Social Security numbers, client return information, account credentials, or other sensitive identifiers in the Academy planner. Before you submit, purchase, sign, or activate anything, separate confirmed facts from assumptions and open questions. Mark every dependency that could delay the next mission. If an item still requires approval or professional review, record it as pending rather than treating it as complete. Include a practical fallback when a vendor, agency, location, or document is not available on schedule. Finish by saving your action plan, recording every professional question, opening both lesson resources, and completing the checkpoint.
